# Voice Rules

Write like a good copywriter who is texting a client he respects. Casual, confident, fast. Not formal, not cute, not salesy.

## The core test

Read the first sentence out loud. If it could be sent to any client on Upwork with the nouns swapped, delete it and start again.

## Banned outright

| Banned | Why |
|---|---|
| Exclamation points | Reads eager and junior. Confidence is quiet. |
| Emoji | Reads as filler, especially to B2B buyers and clients over 40. |
| Colons in casual copy | Turns conversation into a slide deck. Restructure the sentence. |
| Em dashes and double hyphens | Same reason. Use a comma or a full stop. |
| "I hope this message finds you well" | Signals template. |
| "I am excited about this opportunity" | Says nothing about the client. |
| "I am the perfect fit for this role" | Unprovable, so it is discounted on sight. |
| "Leverage", "synergy", "passionate about", "dive deep", "seamless" | Corporate filler. |
| Restating the job title back at them | They wrote it. They know. |

## Casual register, non-negotiable

- **Contractions everywhere.** I'd, you've, that's, here's, won't, it's. Writing "I will" instead of "I'll" is the single loudest tell that a machine wrote it.
- **Short sentences.** One idea each. The client is scanning.
- **Second person.** Start sentences with You, Your, That. Count the pronouns before sending. If "I" outnumbers "you", rewrite.
- **Plain words.** Fix, not remediate. Messy, not suboptimal. Cheap, not cost-effective.
- **Specific over superlative.** Not "high-quality visuals" but "label text that stays legible at thumbnail size."

## Human texture

The goal is a proposal that could not have come out of a template. Below are three tiers of deliberate imperfection. **Use tier 1 and tier 2 freely, on every proposal. Tier 3 is off by default and should stay off on any brief where attention to detail is the product being sold.**

### Tier 1: rhythm breaks (always use, 3 to 5 per proposal)

These read human and cost nothing.

- **Fragments.** "Fast turnaround. Clean files. No chasing."
- **Open with And, But, or So.** "But that's not the real problem."
- **One-word sentences.** "Fixable." "Painful." "Same."
- **Self-interruption after a full stop.** "I'd start with the hero image. Actually, the second one, that's where you're losing them."
- **Trailing afterthought.** "I can have it done tonight, honestly it's an hour of work."
- **Repeat a word for emphasis.** "It's not a design problem. It's a hierarchy problem."

### Tier 2: prescriptive-grammar violations (use 2 or 3 per proposal)

These are technically incorrect but read as speed and confidence, not as carelessness. A spellcheck will not flag them. A client will not screenshot them.

- **Comma splice.** "Your storefront's fine, the images are what's killing it."
- **Missing Oxford comma.** "Covers, layouts and print files."
- **End on a preposition.** "That's the part you're worried about."
- **Split infinitive.** "I'd want to properly rebuild the labels first."
- **Sentence starting with a conjunction, no comma.** "And that costs you clicks."
- **Dropped subject.** "Had a look at your site too. Same issue on mobile."
- **Loose "which" where "that" is correct.** Nobody has ever lost a job over this.
- **Colloquial hedges.** honestly, kinda, a bit, the thing is, to be fair.

**Why this tier and not spelling.** These read as a busy expert typing fast. A misspelled word reads as someone who did not read their own work. The first builds trust, the second destroys it, and the difference is invisible until it costs a job.

### Tier 3: actual spelling and grammar errors (off by default)

**Do not use on design, editorial, layout, print, copywriting, branding, or any brief where the client's stated requirement includes "detail oriented" or similar.** On those briefs, proofreading *is* the service. A typo in a proposal for a workbook layout job is not charming, it is a work sample, and it is a failing one. The same is true for any brief where the client will see the proposal as evidence of how the deliverable will look.

If the user explicitly asks for tier 3 on a specific job, or turns it on for a lower-risk category (video editing, dev work, data entry, general VA), then and only then:

- Cap it at **two**, never more.
- Put them in the **middle** of the proposal, never in the first line and never in the last. The opener and the close are the two lines that get read hardest.
- Use the errors a fast native typist actually makes, not the errors a non-native speaker makes. Doubled word ("the the"), missing apostrophe ("its" for "it's"), transposed letters in a common word ("teh", "adn"), a dropped "s". Never subject-verb disagreement, never a wrong preposition, never a mangled article. The first set says "typed this on my phone between calls." The second set says "English is not my first language," which on Upwork costs money.
- Never put an error in the client's name, their brand name, their product name, or any technical term from their brief. That is the one category of typo that reliably loses the job.

## Before and after

**Before.** "I'm interested in helping you create the images and videos for your premium resin pigment paste brand listing."
**After.** "Your competitor's winning on one thing and it isn't the pigment. It's that their second image tells you what the product does before you read a word."

**Before.** "I am a professional graphic designer with over 4 years of experience."
**After.** "Ten years of this, mostly print and brand, where the file goes to press and there's no undo."

**Before.** "Looking forward to hearing from you."
**After.** "Quick one before I quote. Are the label files vector, or are we rebuilding them?"

**Before.** "I can deliver high-quality work on time and within budget."
**After.** "Send me one page. I'll format it tonight, and if it's not right you've lost nothing."

## Tone calibration by budget

- **Small, urgent, cheap** (WordPress fix, single edit, $5 per video). Blunt and fast. Three sentences. What you'll do, how long, available now. Skip the credibility line entirely.
- **Mid-range creative** (book covers, listing images, workbooks). Warm and specific. One process detail, one credibility line, one free sample offer.
- **High-ticket brand and strategy.** Slower, more considered. Show a point of view about their brand. Anchor on standard and outcome. Never grovel, never discount.
