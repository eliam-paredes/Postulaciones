# The Winning Proposal Corpus

Every proposal below actually won the job. Each entry has the brief, the proposal as sent, and the extractable move.

**How to use this file.** Find the closest job type, steal the *move*, not the words. If a client ever received one of these, sending it again is a liability. Read section 22 at the bottom before finalizing anything.

## 1. YouTube intro slide, brand and motion

**Brief.** Intro slide for a YouTube channel called Body of Art. Audio script already done, 44 seconds. Client wanted earthy, feminine, elegant.

**Sent.**
> Hi Kalea,
>
> Your vision for the Body of Art YouTube intro really resonates with me, especially the earthy, feminine, and elegant vibe you are aiming for. I would love to bring that feeling to life through a visually captivating intro slide that complements your 44-second audio perfectly.
>
> With experience designing YouTube visuals that not only look beautiful but also hold attention, I focus on balanced composition, color palettes, and motion that align with the tone of your brand. For Body of Art, I imagine soft organic textures, refined typography, and a flowing visual rhythm that immediately draws viewers in.
>
> I can develop a mood board to lock in the aesthetic, design a custom intro slide that pairs seamlessly with your audio, and deliver it in high resolution optimized for YouTube's format.
>
> Here are some examples from my portfolio. [3 relevant links]
>
> Looking forward to discussing your vision further.

**The move.** The client handed over three adjectives. Do not compliment the adjectives, *translate them into craft decisions*. Earthy becomes organic texture. Feminine becomes refined type. Elegant becomes flowing rhythm. That translation is the proof of competence, and it takes one sentence.

**Also note.** The 44 seconds is quoted back. Quoting the client's own number proves the brief was read.

## 2. Amazon AI listing images, hard application filter

**Brief.** Supplement brand. AI-generated lifestyle and feature images plus a 7-panel A+ module. Seven numbered application requirements including a mandatory opening sentence, an instruction NOT to send Amazon examples, and a request for a fixed bid.

**Sent.**
> Hi Matthew
>
> I confirm I have access to the Harmonize tool in Photoshop.
>
> I can help you design high-converting Amazon listing images using MidJourney, Kontext Flux, and Photoshop. By blending AI generation with expert retouching, I will deliver photorealistic visuals that highlight your supplement's benefits, look authentic, and meet Amazon's standards.
>
> My background includes working with e-commerce and wellness brands where I optimized product imagery, boosted engagement, and created polished A+ content. I focus on turning AI outputs into retail-ready images with clean detailing and realistic textures.
>
> Would you like me to also provide a few lifestyle variations so you can test which one connects best with your target audience?

**The move.** The required sentence goes **first, verbatim, before the greeting is even finished**. When a client builds a filter into the post, passing the filter *is* round one. Answer every numbered item, in their numbering, in their order. Do not send what they told you not to send.

**Also note.** The close offers A/B variations. That reframes the hire from a cost into a test, which is how e-commerce clients already think.

## 3. ADHD workbook layout

**Brief.** Rough drafts to polished workbook pages. 10 chapters, 100-plus pages. Formatting possibly in scope.

**Sent.**
> This sounds like a project I would love to dive into. I specialize in turning raw content into clean, engaging layouts, especially for educational and mental wellness materials.
>
> I focus on visual clarity, flow, and accessibility, which is important for ADHD audiences. If you would like, I can share workbook samples and walk you through my design approach.
>
> Would love to hear more about your vision for tone and style.

**The move.** Name the constraint the *audience* imposes. ADHD means cognitive load. Elderly means contrast and type size. Kids means hierarchy and negative space. Dyslexia means typeface and line spacing. One sentence doing this separates a designer from a decorator, and almost nobody writes it.

## 4. Amazon book bestseller optimization

**Brief.** Get a book to Amazon Best Seller status. Keyword optimization and promotional tactics.

**Sent.**
> Hi Pamela
>
> Your book isn't just another listing, it's a story waiting to be discovered. But if the right readers aren't seeing it, how will it ever hit that bestseller mark?
>
> I specialize in crafting visually compelling presentations that do more than just look good, they persuade, captivate, and sell. I can transform your book's marketing assets into high-converting visuals without cutting a single word from your message.
>
> Want a quick sample redesign of your current book cover promo?

**The move.** Open with the stake, then a question that answers itself. Works on emotional, identity-driven products (books, personal brands, art). Would fail on a WordPress fix.

**Also note.** "Without cutting a single word from your message" preempts the author's real fear, which is a designer mangling their work.

## 5. Multichannel listing optimization

**Brief.** Established jewelry seller. Update listings across Amazon, eBay, Walmart, and a new Shopify store. Make them consistent. 10 to 20 hours a week, ongoing.

**Sent.**
> Inconsistent listings lose trust, let's fix that fast.
>
> You've already built the legacy, now it's time your listings reflect it. As an Amazon and multichannel specialist who has worked with brands across platforms, I will revamp your content, optimizing your A+ visuals, videos, and descriptions without cutting the story short.
>
> Would you be open to a free mini audit of one listing to see the quality firsthand?

**The move.** Six-word opening line that names the business consequence, not the task. "Inconsistent listings" is the symptom, "lose trust" is the cost. Clients buy the cost, not the symptom.

**Also note.** "You've already built the legacy" credits them before proposing changes. On established businesses, criticism without credit reads as an insult.

## 6. Rush video edit, vague brief

**Brief.** 2-minute video, urgent, details after hiring, attach samples.

**Sent.**
> Eugene, I see you're looking for a quick turnaround on a 2-minute video, I'm all in.
>
> Can you share a bit more about the style and vibe you're going for? Is it more cinematic, fast-paced, or something else?
>
> Also, how tight is the deadline? Just so I can prioritize and make sure everything's ready when you need it.
>
> Feel free to see my video portfolio here. [link]

**The move.** When the brief is empty, *good questions are the portfolio*. Three tight questions beat three paragraphs of claims. Asking about the deadline in order to prioritize also signals capacity without boasting.

## 11. A4 magazine advert, print

**Brief.** A4 magazine ad for a UK caravan business, styled like premium car ads. Client had reference pins, a favorite concept, and wanted an older wealthy couple instead of a surfer. "ONLY apply if you can produce adverts of this quality."

**Sent.**
> Hello Sam,
>
> We're ready to create an attractive and effective A4 page design that resembles leading car adverts, and we've taken note of your preferred concept, the top one you mentioned.
>
> We'll include a picture of an older, wealthy-looking happy couple instead of the surfer, as per your request. We'll utilize your logos and assets and deliver the design in editable PSD format.
>
> I'm Fahad, a professional graphic designer. You can see my portfolio at [link]. My hourly rate is $18.
>
> I believe in long-term work. If you're available, let's schedule a meeting and get started immediately.

**The move.** **Confirm the deliverable format explicitly.** Editable PSD. Press-ready PDF. Bleed and crop marks. Clients who have been burned on print care enormously about this and virtually nobody mentions it. It is the cheapest possible trust signal.

**Also note.** Every specific instruction in the brief is checked off, one by one. On "ONLY apply if" posts, visible compliance is the pitch.

## 12. Explainer videos, tool-specific

**Brief.** Several 2-minute explainer videos a month. Voiceover, stock footage, sound effects. Canva and Jitter required. A Zendesk video given as the reference.

**Sent.**
> Hi Angelo.
>
> I can create engaging 2-minute videos that incorporate voiceovers and stock footage, using Canva and Jitter to enhance visual appeal.
>
> With a strong portfolio that showcases my work, I'm confident I can bring your vision to life. I pay close attention to detail, ensuring the final product tells a compelling story while conveying your message.
>
> I'm ready to start by introducing a FREE SAMPLE to see if we are a right fit.

Plus, when asked to describe the reference video:

> The video gives a simple overview of how Zendesk helps businesses manage customer service more effectively. It shows how the platform brings together email, chat, and phone into one dashboard, making it easier for agents to handle tickets faster.
>
> The editing style is clean and straightforward, using a mix of screen captures and voiceovers to explain the features without getting too technical.

**The move.** When a reference link is given, **watch it and describe it back**. The description is the whole audition. It proves you looked, and it proves you can read a style and name it, which is the actual skill being hired.

## 13. Photoshop retouching

**Brief.** Enhance and manipulate a specific image. Color correction, retouching, possible background removal.

**Sent.**
> Dave, getting the perfect balance of color, detail, and realism is where Photoshop magic happens. The key? Enhancing without over-processing. Sharp yet natural.
>
> I specialize in high-impact retouching that elevates an image without losing authenticity. Whether it's color correction, retouching, or background cleanup, I'll refine your image with a seamless, polished look.
>
> Want a quick preview? I can enhance a section first so you see the quality before we proceed. What's the main focus of the edit?

**The move.** Name the craft *tension*, not the craft. Every retoucher can retouch. Only a good one knows the danger is over-processing. Naming the failure mode of your own discipline is the fastest credibility signal that exists, and it works in every field. For layout it is cramming. For motion it is over-animating. For copy it is over-writing.

**Also note.** "I can enhance a section first" is the free sample scaled down to almost zero cost.

## 14. Premiere template assembly at volume

**Brief.** Drag 1 to 3 minute videos into a Premiere project with animation templates, set to music, pans and zooms, good composition. 10 minutes each, $5 per video, possibly for months.

**Sent.**
> Hello Scott,
>
> I noticed you need to drag 1 to 3-minute videos into a Premiere project with animation templates. To ensure good composition with pans and zooms, I can arrange the videos to the music within 10 minutes each. My rate for each video is $5.
>
> I have reviewed the rough video instructions you provided and I am confident I can deliver. You can find examples of my previous work on my Vimeo and YouTube. [links]
>
> Let's discuss further details. I am available via Zoom or any platform you prefer.

**The move.** Accept their number out loud. On a rate-specified job, haggling in the proposal loses. Restate their constraints back at them (10 minutes, $5, pans and zooms) so it is unmistakable that the terms were read and accepted. Then win on the long tail, since this one said "several months or longer."

## 16. Amazon storefront rebuild with a trigger word

**Brief.** Client's own words: the US store "looks terrible and needs fixing." Wanted a clean contemporary look, 5 main products plus bundles uploaded, tips on making the storefront stickier, and instructed applicants to use the word "ninja" in the response.

**Sent.**
> Hi Paul,
>
> "Ninja" here.
>
> I understand you need the storefront to look cleaner and more professional. I will design and upload for you. I recently designed a storefront for a client selling in the US, here's the link. [live link]
>
> I can start now and complete the upload by tomorrow. Your storefront looks messy at the moment. Some images are not optimized according to the dimensions. I just had a look at your website too.
>
> I can complete the task within the $45 budget. Quick question, are you currently running PPC or any other ads for traffic on your storefront?
>
> Are you available for a quick meeting right now?

**The move.** This is the strongest proposal in the corpus and it is worth studying line by line.
- Trigger word used immediately, in the second line.
- An *unsolicited specific fault* is named. Images not optimized to dimensions. The client did not ask for a diagnosis, and getting one for free is irresistible.
- Their own website is checked too, unprompted.
- The budget is accepted explicitly, which removes the negotiation anxiety.
- The close is a **commercial question, not a design question.** Asking about PPC proves this is someone thinking about traffic and conversion, not just pixels. That question is worth more than the rest of the proposal.

**The move to reuse everywhere.** For any e-commerce brief, look at the actual asset before writing and say one true thing that is wrong with it. Then ask one question a marketer would ask.

## 17. Resin pigment brand, listing from zero

**Brief.** Premium resin pigment paste brand, listing from scratch, competitor link provided, client has photos of bottles, labels, box.

**Sent.**
> Hello,
>
> I'm interested in helping you create the images and videos for your listing. Do you have better images of your product? What colors do you prefer in the design? Are there specific features you want highlighted?
>
> Based on the competitor link you shared, I can create attention-grabbing listing images, A+ content, and videos to showcase your product.
>
> I estimate 40 to 50 hours for all the work at an hourly rate of $18. I'm happy to provide a FREE sample image to showcase my expertise.

**The move.** Estimate the hours out loud. Naming a realistic number ("40 to 50 hours") signals someone who has actually done this before and is not guessing. Vagueness on scope reads as inexperience.

**The weakness.** The opening ("I'm interested in helping you create...") is the weakest in the corpus. It won anyway, but it would be stronger as an observation about the competitor listing they linked. Something like: "Your competitor is winning on their second image, not their first, and it is costing you clicks." **Do not copy this opener.**

## 18. Figma to WordPress build

**Brief.** Figma file with 5 pages, plus cart and checkout, plus a quantity-based pricing table called "Engros" on each product page. Lightweight builder, LSCache speed optimization, mobile responsive per Figma guides.

**Sent.**
> Hi,
>
> Your project sounds clear and well thought out, which makes this exciting to dive into. Can you share your Figma file with me?
>
> For the Engros functionality on the product page, how detailed do you want the table to be?
>
> For the lightweight builder, are you leaning towards something like Oxygen or Bricks?
>
> And with speed optimization using LSCache, do you already have a hosting setup that supports it?
>
> Looking forward to bringing this to life.

**The move.** On a well-specified technical brief, **do not sell. Ask the questions only a practitioner would know to ask.** Oxygen or Bricks. Does your hosting support LSCache. The questions *are* the credential. A generic applicant physically cannot write them.

**Sent.**

>
> I saw the video and I'm confident I can make all the necessary edits.

**The move.** They said watch it. So watch it, then say something only a viewer could say. When a brief contains a "do this first" test, most applicants skip it, which means passing the test alone puts you in the top handful.

## 20. What every winner has in common

Six elements. Aim for all six on jobs worth the effort.

1. **Personalization.** Client's name. Client's own vocabulary mirrored back. Client's actual asset referenced.
2. **Their pain restated in your words.** Not "I can do this." Instead, here is what is wrong and what it is costing you.
3. **Specific deliverables, not adjectives.** Mood board, then intro slide, then optimized export. Not "beautiful, engaging visuals."
4. **Confidence without overselling.** "That's exactly what I do." "I specialize in." No superlatives, no begging.
5. **Risk reduction.** A free sample, a mini audit, one enhanced section, one formatted page. This is the single highest-converting element in the entire corpus. It appears in most of the wins.
6. **A closing question the client can answer in one line.** Never "looking forward to hearing from you."

Plus one meta-pattern: **tone flexes with budget.** Three lines on a $45 fix. Storytelling on a book brand. Read the money and match the register.

## 21. The four failure patterns to avoid

These are the weaknesses visible in the corpus. Fix them every time.

1. **Soft openers.** "I'm interested in helping you create images" is dead on arrival. Replace with an observation, a consequence, or the client's own words. Compare: "Let's make your resin pigments outshine the competitor you linked, starting with the image they got wrong."
2. **Portfolio links without context.** A bare Behance URL asks the client to do the work of understanding it. Give each link one line: what was broken, what was fixed. "The A+ rebuild for a Turkish soap brand. The fix was mostly hierarchy and thumbnail legibility."
3. **Passive calls to action.** "Let me know" puts the burden on them. Use "Shall I send one formatted page tonight?" or "Do you want me to start with your best seller first?"
4. **No results named.** Every proposal says what will be done. Almost none say what happened last time. Add a number when a real one exists. Never invent one.

## 22. Final rules

- **Steal the move, never the sentence.** These exact words have been sent. Reusing them across clients is how a portfolio of wins turns into a template that reads like a template.
- **The failure mode goes in line one.** Everything after it is evidence.
- **When in doubt, ask a better question and offer a free look.** That combination has won more of these than any adjective ever will.
