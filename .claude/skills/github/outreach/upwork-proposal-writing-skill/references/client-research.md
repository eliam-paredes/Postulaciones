# Client Research

Research is what makes the first line impossible to write for anyone else. Do it before drafting.

## 1. The product itself

Pull whatever the client pointed at: their website, Amazon listing, storefront, YouTube channel, Figma file, or the competitor link they shared. Then find **one true, specific, useful observation**.

Good observations sound like this:

- "Your hero image is doing two jobs at once, so it does neither at thumbnail size."
- "The bundles are not surfaced anywhere above the fold, which is probably costing you your highest-margin SKU."
- "Your competitor's listing wins on the second image, not the first. It shows the product mid-use."
- "The footer is fine on desktop and collapses at 768 pixels."

Bad observations sound like this:

- "Your website looks great."
- "I love your brand."
- "There is room for improvement."

One real observation is worth more than the entire rest of the proposal. If nothing true and specific can be found, say nothing rather than flattering.

## 2. The Upwork profile signals

If visible, note these and let them shape the pitch:

| Signal | What it means | How to pitch |
|---|---|---|
| High total spend, many past hires | Experienced buyer, knows what good looks like | Be precise and professional. Skip the hard sell. Pitch the relationship. |
| Payment unverified, no hires yet | New or possibly not serious | Keep effort proportionate. Short proposal. Do not build a free deck. |
| High hire rate | Actually hires, worth the effort | Invest in the proposal. Offer the free sample. |
| Low hire rate, many posts | Fishing for ideas or prices | Short proposal. Do not give away strategy for free. |
| Several parallel open jobs | Building a team or scaling | Pitch capacity and repeat work, not a one-off. |
| Rehire rate high | Keeps freelancers | Emphasize reliability and long-term fit. |
| Budget far below market | Either a test or a bad client | Either decline, or bid honestly and explain the tradeoff rather than racing to the bottom. |

## 3. The tell in the wording

- Repeated emphasis on deadlines means they were ghosted before. Lead with reliability and turnaround.
- "Must be detail oriented" means the last person shipped sloppy files. Lead with proofing and file hygiene.
- "Only apply if you can produce this quality" means they got junk. Lead with a matched sample.
- A trigger word requirement ("use the word ninja") means they are filtering copy-paste. Use it in the first line.
- "Details provided after hiring" means the brief is not written yet. Ask two or three sharp scoping questions instead of pretending to know.

## 4. What to do with it

The research goes in the **first two sentences**, not in a paragraph near the bottom. It is the hook. Everything after it is just proof that the hook was not luck.
