# Portfolio and Credibility Anchors

## Selection rule

**Always include the general portfolio link:** https://www.behance.net/fahadqaseem

It goes in every proposal, at the bottom, as a bare line. It is the fallback that works when no category link fits.

On top of that, pick **two or three category links that match the job type**, and write one line under each saying what the problem was and what the fix was. A link with context beats five links without.

If the brief or the client's context suggests attachments are safer than links (corporate clients, clients who say "attach examples", or briefs where links may get stripped), attach files and still include the Behance link as the catch-all.

## Credibility anchors

See `positioning.md` for the credibility line itself. This section covers which anchor fits which brief.

Deploy at most one or two per proposal, and only when relevant.

- **DKNY.** Use as the signal of brand discipline and professional pedigree. Best on brand identity, fashion, retail, editorial, and premium print briefs. Do not use it on a $30 WordPress footer fix, where it just looks like posturing.
- **Nonprofit work.** Use on mission-driven, community, education, health, and public-sector briefs. Signals that the work is not purely commercial and that budget-conscious constraints are familiar.
- **Long-run agency experience.** Co-founded a digital branding and e-commerce studio, 200-plus clients. Use when the client is worried about reliability, volume, or the ability to run a process rather than a one-off.
- **Technical depth.** M.S. in Computer Science, AI/ML engineering, IEEE-published. Use on AI image generation, automation, and anything where the client is nervous about the tooling. This is a genuine differentiator against pure designers on AI-heavy briefs, so lead with it there.
